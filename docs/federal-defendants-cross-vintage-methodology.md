# Research: ABS Federal Defendants cross-vintage methodology, 2021–22 to 2024–25

## Summary

The four releases share a stable collection core: a **finalised defendant case** (person or organisation) charged under Commonwealth legislation in state/territory Higher, Magistrates’ or Children’s Courts, with each separately finalised case counted and transfers between court levels excluded unless a table says otherwise. They are not, however, one fully interchangeable statistical family. The 2022–23 release introduced and broadly backcast the Sentence Type Classification 2023, and the 2024–25 release changed offence output from ANZSOC 2011 to ANZSOC 2023 and concorded earlier offence data; both are semantic boundaries requiring explicit family/version splits.

## Findings

### 1. Scope, counting unit and transfer handling

1. **The unit is a finalised defendant case, not a unique person.** A defendant may be a person or organisation. Each case separately finalised during the financial year is counted, so the same defendant may appear more than once when separate cases are finalised. The collection is a subset of *Criminal Courts, Australia*. [2022–23 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2022-23) [2024–25 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2024-25)
2. **Transfers are normally excluded from the finalised-defendant count.** All four release descriptions say transfers between court levels are excluded unless specified. This prevents a transfer being treated as a completed case in the headline total. However, the release summary's court-level display explicitly **includes transfers**, so court-level counts can exceed and must not be summed to the transfer-excluding all-courts total. For example, the 2021–22 summary reports 869 Higher + 7,798 Magistrates’ + 268 Children’s = 8,935 court-level events against 8,092 headline finalised defendants. [2021–22 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2021-22)
3. **Federal scope is statutory, not prosecutor-based.** In scope are charges against Commonwealth legislation, whether prosecuted by state/territory police, AFP, Commonwealth or state DPPs, ASIC, ATO or another authority. State/territory-only cases are outside this product. National laws enacted through state/territory legislation have been excluded since 2016–17 for comparability; Australian Road Rules and their enacting laws are also excluded even where a jurisdiction identifies them as federal. [2021–22 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2021-22)

**Implementation consequence:** store `counting_unit=separately_finalised_defendant_case` and a separate `transfer_policy`. A transfer-inclusive court-level observation is a different universe and requires a separate family from the transfer-excluding headline/all-courts observation.

### 2. Organisations, sex and age

1. **Organisations are defendants but not people.** The sex categories are male, female, organisations, other and unknown. The `other` category is not published. A model must not silently convert “defendants” to “persons.” [2024–25 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2024-25)
2. **Age is measured at finalisation, not offence date.** A defendant’s age is calculated at the date the matter(s) was finalised. Organisation and unknown-age records do not belong in person-age denominators. [2022–23 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2022-23) [2024–25 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2024-25)
3. **Published summary counts demonstrate the distinction.** Organisations were 79, 126 and included again in later vintages; median age and male/female shares describe person characteristics, whereas the headline total includes organisations. [2021–22 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2021-22) [2022–23 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2022-23)

**Implementation consequence:** use separate `entity_type` and `sex` dimensions; do not map organisation to unknown sex. Split all-entity defendant totals from person-only age/sex measures.

### 3. Population bases and contextual denominators

These Federal Defendants workbooks publish counts and shares rather than the ERP-based defendant rates used by *Criminal Courts, Australia*. The release pages contextualise federal defendants as a share of **all finalised defendants in state and territory criminal courts in the same financial year**: 8,092 of 488,936 (2021–22), 9,232 of 533,971 (2022–23), 8,723 of 515,460 (2023–24), and 9,158 of 521,632 (2024–25). Those totals are collection denominators, not Estimated Resident Population. [2021–22 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2021-22) [2022–23 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2022-23) [2023–24 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2023-24) [2024–25 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/latest-release)

**Implementation consequence:** do not attach an ERP denominator to these observations by inheritance from the parent Criminal Courts collection. Percentage measures need their workbook-defined denominator (all federal defendants, adjudicated defendants, or guilty defendants), which is part of measure identity.

### 4. Geography and court levels

1. Data are available for states/territories and Australia and cover Higher Courts (Supreme and District/County), Magistrates’ Courts and Children’s Courts. The source is annual administrative data supplied by courts administration or statistical agencies. [2023–24 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2023-24)
2. ABS “Australia” includes Other Territories. Cocos (Keeling) Islands and Christmas Island finalisations are included with Western Australia where applicable; Jervis Bay Territory and Norfolk Island are excluded. [2021–22 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2021-22)
3. “Higher Courts” is a court-level rollup, not the Commonwealth Federal Court. The collection concerns criminal cases in state/territory court systems.

### 5. Offence classification and Federal Offence Group

1. **2021–22 through 2023–24 offence outputs are ANZSOC 2011.** The principal offence is an ANZSOC category; the separately published **Federal Offence Group** is an additional grouping tailored to Commonwealth legislation (for example communications), assigned from the selected principal offence. It is not an ANZSOC level and must have its own code system/version. [2021–22 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2021-22) [2022–23 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2022-23)
2. **2024–25 outputs are ANZSOC 2023.** The release explicitly states that offence data in earlier releases were ANZSOC 2011, while the 2024–25 publication presents ANZSOC 2023 and concords historical values to provide a time series. Labels therefore change materially—for example “Harassment and threatening behaviour” becomes “Acts that threaten, harass, or control,” and broader categories such as “Fraud and related offences,” “Offences against government services,” and “Sexual offences” appear in the release narrative. [2024–25 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/latest-release)
3. ANZSOC 2023 is a fourth edition updated for legislation, language and social change, though ABS retained structure/content where possible to support continuity. “Where possible” is not equivalence. [ANZSOC 2023](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/classifications/australian-and-new-zealand-standard-offence-classification-anzsoc/2023)
4. The official detailed crosswalk is [ANZSOC concordances 2011 and 2023.xlsx](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/classifications/australian-and-new-zealand-standard-offence-classification-anzsoc/2023/ANZSOC%20concordances%202011%20and%202023.xlsx). Concordance can be one-to-many or many-to-one; it is mapping evidence, not permission to relabel native 2011 observations as native 2023.
5. The 2024–25 methodology says principal offence is ANZSOC 2023 **from 2023–24** and prior years are concorded from original ANZSOC 2011. It also records that minor refinements to preliminary 2023–24 ANZSOC 2023 coding changed defendant counts across Divisions. Thus the 2023–24 offence values republished in the 2024–25 workbook are not necessarily identical to the standalone 2023–24 release. [2024–25 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2024-25)

### 6. Principal federal offence selection

For a single federal charge, that charge is principal. With multiple federal charges, ABS selects in this order:

1. charge in the highest-ranked method-of-finalisation group;
2. if tied, charge with the most serious sentence under the applicable Sentence Type Classification;
3. if still tied, the offence ranked most serious by the National Offence Index (NOI).

The NOI is an ordinal seriousness ranking of ANZSOC groups. [2024–25 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2024-25) [ANZSOC 2023 NOI](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/classifications/australian-and-new-zealand-standard-offence-classification-anzsoc/2023/national-offence-index-noi)

The release warns that principal-offence data before 2018–19 were based only on method of finalisation and NOI; from 2018–19 sentence type and length/amount are included. This is a methodological break inside long workbook time series, independent of the ANZSOC version break. [2023–24 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2023-24)

**Implementation consequence:** principal-offence observations need `principal_selection_version` (`pre_2018_19_mof_noi` versus `2018_19_plus_mof_sentence_noi`). They should not be treated as an unqualified same-method series.

### 7. Sentence classification break

1. The 2021–22 release used the previous Sentence Type Classification.
2. An updated **Sentence Type Classification 2023** was introduced in the 2022–23 release. It describes sentences for guilty outcomes and orders them by seriousness. ABS backcast the new broad principal-sentence groups to earlier years on a best-endeavours basis, “where possible,” and notes consistency issues. [2022–23 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2022-23)
3. The 2023–24 release reiterates that earlier principal-sentence results were broadly mapped from the old classification; the current classification was designed to reflect compound-sentence severity and improve comparability. [2023–24 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2023-24)
4. Broad output labels include custody in a correctional institution, custody in the community, community supervision/work orders or moderate community penalties, monetary orders/fines, good behaviour including bonds, and nominal/other penalties. Label similarity across workbooks does not establish detailed-category equivalence.

**Implementation consequence:** split `sentence_native_pre_2022_23` from `sentence_stc2023_current`; historical values mapped/backcast into STC 2023 require a third provenance state (`stc2023_backcast_from_old`). Same-family membership is defensible only for explicitly mapped broad groups and only with the mapping flag retained.

### 8. Confidentiality, perturbation and marker legends

ABS uses perturbation: small random adjustments are made to published cells to protect confidentiality. A cell may differ slightly from the unperturbed value, the same underlying count may differ across tables, and components may not sum to totals. Perturbation is designed not to impair the value of the statistics but means exact additivity is not a valid quality test. [2023–24 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2023-24) [2024–25 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2024-25)

Workbook markers must remain typed states, not parsed as zero. Across ABS court tables the relevant legends are:

- `–` — nil or rounded to zero;
- `na` — not available;
- `..` — not applicable;
- `np` / `n.p.` — not published (including confidentiality/reliability reasons where stated);
- footnote letters/symbols — table-specific qualifications;
- `r` where present — revised.

The unpublished `other` sex category is an example of deliberate non-publication, not zero. [2023–24 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2023-24)

**Implementation consequence:** preserve raw display marker, parsed null reason and footnote references. Never coerce `np`, `na` or `..` to zero; retain `–` as a display state even if a downstream numeric representation uses zero.

### 9. Corrections, revisions and release history

- No “Corrections” block was present on any of the four official release pages when inspected; absence of a block is not a guarantee that files will never be replaced. Pin year-specific URLs and record retrieval timestamp/hash in implementation.
- 2022–23’s stated historical change is the updated sentence classification and broad backcast. [2022–23 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2022-23)
- 2023–24 states “Not applicable to this release” under history of changes and “There are no revisions for 2023–24” in methodology. [2023–24 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2023-24)
- 2024–25’s history states the ANZSOC 2023 principal-offence transition and concordance; its revisions note minor refinement of preliminary 2023–24 ANZSOC 2023 coding, changing Division counts. [2024–25 methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/federal-defendants-australia-methodology/2024-25)
- COVID-19 restrictions and later backlog resolution affected volumes over these years. This is a comparability caveat, not a definition change. [2022–23 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2022-23) [2023–24 release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/crime-and-justice/federal-defendants-australia/2023-24)

## Required family splits

| Severity | Split | Why same-family membership is unsafe |
|---|---|---|
| **Blocker** | ANZSOC 2011 native offence vs ANZSOC 2023 native offence vs 2024–25 concorded history | Categories, codes and labels changed; concordance is not identity. 2023–24 is especially dual-status because the standalone release is 2011-based while the next release republishes/refines it as 2023-based. |
| **Blocker** | Pre-2022–23 native sentence classification vs STC 2023 current vs STC 2023 backcast | New hierarchy/severity treatment and best-efforts broad mapping alter category meaning and potentially principal-offence selection. |
| **Blocker** | Transfer-excluding finalised defendants vs transfer-inclusive court-level events | Different universes; summing court levels does not reproduce the headline count. |
| **High** | Pre-2018–19 principal-offence selection vs 2018–19 onward selection | Later selection adds sentence type and length/amount before NOI tie-breaking. |
| **High** | All defendants (persons + organisations) vs person-only age/sex measures | Entity population differs; organisation is not unknown sex and has no person age. |
| **High** | All finalised / adjudicated / guilty-outcome denominators | Sentence shares apply to guilty outcomes; judgement shares to adjudicated finalisations; headline offence shares generally exclude transfers. |
| **Medium** | ANZSOC principal-offence group vs Federal Offence Group | They are distinct classification systems even when derived from the same principal charge. |

Renumbering alone, filename capitalisation, removal of the year suffix, and download-block redesign are **not** semantic splits. They are source-layout versions.

## Recommended durable observation keys

At minimum retain: `release_vintage`, `reference_period`, `workbook_url`, `workbook_hash`, `guide_url`, `table_number`, `table_title`, `counting_unit`, `entity_type`, `transfer_policy`, `court_level`, `geography`, `outcome_universe`, `measure_denominator`, `offence_classification` and version, `offence_code`, `federal_offence_group_version`, `principal_selection_version`, `sentence_classification` and mapping status, `value`, `display_marker`, `perturbation=true`, `footnotes`, and `revision_status`.

## Gaps and residual risks

1. **Workbook legends and table-level footnotes vary.** The marker list above reflects the official ABS court-table conventions and methodology evidence, but ingestion must capture each sheet’s exact legend rather than assume every marker occurs in every vintage.
2. **Guide content is visual workbook metadata.** Exact item-to-table mappings should be extracted directly from each pinned guide during implementation; this review attests the file URLs and table ranges, not every cell coordinate.
3. **Latest-release mutability.** The 2024–25 page is a moving alias. Use the year-specific file URLs, save content hashes and archive retrieval timestamps.
4. **No historical file hashes were published on the pages.** A silent ABS file replacement would only be detectable through implementation-side hashing/version capture.

## Review findings

- **blocker:** offence family design — do not merge native ANZSOC 2011, native ANZSOC 2023 and concorded ANZSOC 2023 history.
- **blocker:** sentence family design — do not merge pre-2022–23 native detailed sentences with STC 2023 current/backcast values without mapping provenance.
- **blocker:** universe design — do not merge transfer-inclusive court-level counts with transfer-excluding finalised-defendant totals.
- **high:** preserve the 2018–19 principal-offence selection break and person/organisation distinction.
- **high:** table numbers changed and must not be used as stable semantic identifiers.
